# Visual Planning

Turn creative intent, references and assets into a concrete, coherent, executable visual plan. Plan what viewers will see, how they will understand it, and how the picture develops over time.

Choose the depth of planning for the task. Reuse decisions that already work and focus on unresolved creative questions.

## Start with the viewing experience

Identify what viewers should understand, notice, feel and remember.

Use these goals to choose content, sequence and treatment. Give key visual choices a purpose: communicating information, directing attention, establishing emotion, creating contrast or paying off a setup.

Translate abstract intentions such as “premium,” “striking” or “futuristic” into visible design choices that guide production.

## Develop the user's creative intent

Build on the user's brief, Moments reference clips, other references, brand, assets and confirmed decisions. Identify which features must be preserved and where there is room to explore.

Keep local changes focused on the experience being changed, while considering adjacent pictures, voiceover, music, sound effects (SFX) and overall pacing. Match the scope of the adjustment to the scope of its impact.

## Understand how references work

Analyze the mechanisms in a reference: how subjects appear, attention shifts, information is revealed, actions accumulate and pictures connect.

For Moments, identify what the user wants to preserve: the opening action, camera movement, visual transformation, timing of a title or rhythm against the music. Adapt those features to the current content.

When combining references, decide what each contributes and organize them into a consistent expression. Understand the effect on the viewing experience before changing a feature the user values.

## Make the concept a visible event

Express the core idea through concrete subjects, relationships, actions or changes that viewers can understand through the picture.

For example, “complexity becomes simplicity” can appear as scattered elements organizing into a clear structure. Demonstrate a product capability through an actual operation and its result.

Give text, pictures, voiceover, music and SFX appropriate roles. Voiceover can explain, music can establish emotion and rhythm, and SFX can emphasize actions and events. Decoration and atmosphere support the expression; key claims need a clear visual treatment.

## Turn references into your own visual design

Arrange reference images together and compare their colors, typography, composition and materials. Decide which features suit the project.

Then design representative pictures using the project's real copy, brand elements, product assets and intended aspect ratio. Check whether these choices work together in your own content.

When the direction is unresolved, compare a few materially different treatments. Choose based on communication and overall consistency, then extend the design to the remaining pictures.

## Progressive disclosure

Reveal content progressively as time advances. Use changes in position, size and movement to guide attention through each beat.

Choreograph three kinds of change:

- Visibility: reveal an element or replace a placeholder with content. Establish the layout first, then uncover its parts.
- Position and size: move and scale elements to redistribute the frame. A main element can grow and advance while supporting elements shrink and move aside.
- Viewport: reveal content by scrolling within a container or moving the camera across a larger canvas.

When there is a voice script, coordinate disclosure and emphasis with the narration. Use masking, color, scale and position changes to emphasize what is being discussed. Replace earlier content in place, move it aside or retain it as context. Develop longer passages across connected shots, reusing elements with changes in framing, scale and emphasis. Without a voice script, this narration-led arrangement can be skipped.

## Compose with space and scale

Organize elements through hierarchy, grids, scale and spatial relationships. Use foreground, midground, background, light and shadow to build depth where the content benefits from it.

The canvas can be larger than the frame. Arrange content in a continuous space and explore it through camera movement. Split screens and proportional zones can present complementary content at the same time.

## Design in time

Consider where a picture comes from, what happens, where it ends and where it hands off attention.

Give key actions clear starting states, transformations and ending states. Keep recurring subjects traceable through identity, position or movement. When deliberately breaking continuity, make the break produce a specific effect.

Transitions connect the content and states of adjacent pictures. Use anchor element transitions frequently: choose a traceable element connecting the two shots, then change its position, scale, form or framing to carry the previous shot into the next. Develop the new picture around that anchor.

Establish the mechanism and viewing effect first; decide exact animation parameters when implementation needs them.

## Give the whole piece a progression

Choose an organization that suits the content, such as cause and effect, contrast, gradual revelation, a process demonstration or an emotional build.

Each section should add information, change a state or advance the experience. Repetition can establish rhythm, reinforce an impression or create anticipation; give it a clear purpose.

When a piece relies on a recurring subject or visual thread, decide how it develops and where it pays off. Connect the expectations established at the opening to what follows.

Both structure and asset choices serve the current content. Develop the structure around what the available assets can express, and select assets for what the structure needs. Do not impose a universal narrative formula.

## Balance fast and slow pacing

Set the pace according to each section's purpose. An opening hook, sudden transformation or forceful movement can advance quickly to capture attention. Key information, product demonstrations and emotional landings can slow down so viewers see them clearly.

Create deliberate contrast between fast and slow passages. Slowing down needs a specific purpose: allowing text to be read, showing an operation's result, creating anticipation or emphasizing a moment. Avoid keeping every section at the same speed.

Control pacing through information density, movement speed, cuts, scale and space.

Use camera moves frequently to sustain movement through the shot: push in, pull back, pan or travel through the scene. Changes in the size and position of main and supporting elements can also advance the picture, developing their relationships over time. Align key changes with voiceover keywords, music beats or SFX.

Plan with the speed and pauses of the voiceover, the beats and sections of the music, and the timing of SFX. When duration is constrained, adjust the information load and treatment so the content works within the available time.

## Make uncertain ideas visible

Use concrete pictures or motion to judge key creative choices:

- To assess composition, typography and color, design a representative picture with actual content.
- To assess how two pictures connect, sketch the before, during and after states; make a short rough animation when needed.
- To assess the whole piece's rhythm, arrange sketches or key pictures at their intended durations, add the anticipated voiceover, music and key SFX, and play it back.
- To assess an asset or production method, make a representative segment first.

Keep the features needed for the judgment in the preview. Checking whether a title is readable needs the real copy and display duration; checking a transition needs its movement.

## Resolve decisions with broad consequences first

Prioritize decisions whose changes would affect substantial downstream work, such as the core visual concept, subject design, key transitions and overall pacing.

Resolve these questions with previews concrete enough to judge, then expand into full production. Invest in details once the direction works.

Planning can move back and forth. When new assets, measured durations or production results provide new evidence, revisit the affected decisions and update the related parts.
