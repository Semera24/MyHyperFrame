# Image generation and editing

## Describe the intended picture

State the image's purpose and content. Include subject, setting, framing, viewpoint, lighting, materials, and text where they affect the result.

For a video first frame, depict the shot's starting state and leave room for the intended action. A person walking right needs space to move; a hand picking up an object needs a clear initial spatial relationship to it.

## Combine references

Assign roles: identity or clothing, product shape and details, environment, visual treatment, or composition and pose. Explain how elements interact in the target picture, such as how a person holds a product, which way it faces, and which details must remain visible.

## Edit an existing picture

Specify what changes and what stays. For example: replace the cup with the referenced bottle, adjust the fingers to grip it naturally, and preserve the face, clothing, camera angle, and background.

Decide whether related changes should happen together or in stages. During staged edits, compare against original references and approved results to catch unintended changes to identity, product details, or composition.

## Prepare for animation and compositing

Before animation, inspect appearance, hand-object contact, composition for the upcoming motion, and any required text. Correct relevant defects in the image first.

When later compositing needs separate layers, prepare background and subject assets separately. For a subject that will be keyed out, choose an easily separable background color that does not overlap the subject's colors. This can also apply to separately generated background and subject videos.

## Explore alternatives

Make candidates differ in concrete choices such as framing, viewpoint, lighting, or setting. Preserve established identity, product details, and user requirements across those alternatives.
