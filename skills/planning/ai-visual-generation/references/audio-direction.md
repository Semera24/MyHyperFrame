# Audio direction

Handle voice-over, dialogue, music, and SFX separately. Identify existing audio and what needs generation.

## Voice-over

Narration explains or advances the content and need not come from an on-screen speaker. Use the actual timing of an established narration track to arrange relevant pictures and actions.

When generating narration, specify the words, language, and delivery. Choose the available video or separate speech capability according to the task.

## Dialogue

For on-screen speech, identify the speaker, exact line, and performance. Clearly assign lines in a conversation. Existing dialogue audio provides the timing reference for the performance.

## Music

State the music's purpose, such as atmosphere, rhythmic drive, or a change between sections. Use the actual track to place synchronized actions, cuts, and transitions when music already exists.

Choose generation with the video or later addition according to the task and available capabilities.

## SFX and ambience

Attach effects to events: footsteps, opening a door, an object landing, fabric movement, or a machine starting. Describe synchronization where it matters: the bottle makes a light impact as it touches the table, followed by liquid moving inside.

Use ambience to express space and background activity, such as room tone, rain, traffic, or a distant crowd.

## Review

Listen to the result. Check exact words and speaker attribution, synchronization with action, intelligibility of speech against music and effects, and sound across clip boundaries. When sound needs correction, consider whether the audio can be edited separately while retaining effective pictures.
