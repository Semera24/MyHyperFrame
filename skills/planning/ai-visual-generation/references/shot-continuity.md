# Shot continuity and extension

Choose the generation method for the relationship between shots.

## Continue one take

When extending a shot, consider the tool's video-extension capability to carry forward motion, environment, and camera state.

When using an earlier clip's final frame as the next first frame, choose a clear frame suitable for continuing the action and describe motion direction and speed. One still captures a state, not necessarily the movement that led into it.

## Connect different shots

For continuity across a cut, establish relevant boundary states: character position, orientation and pose; object location and condition; action direction and progress; and environmental details that must carry through.

Use shared character and setting references, separately prepared keyframes, or supported first/last-frame controls as the shot design needs. Ordinary cuts, jump cuts, and montages do not all require shared boundary frames.

## Build a visual transition

Identify the connecting element. To transition from a round object in a hand to a round window, design the outgoing and incoming compositions and explain how shape, position, scale, or motion connects them.

## Use storyboards

A storyboard can communicate shot order, composition, and action changes. State which aspects should guide generation. Use the individual shot image when an actual first frame is needed.

Check that panel borders, labels, arrows, or the grid itself have not leaked into the generated video.

## Review the join

Watch adjacent clips together to inspect pose, position, movement direction, rhythm, and sound at the boundary. Locate the shot or transition that needs correction and retain the effective parts.
