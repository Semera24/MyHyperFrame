# AI visual generation

Translate creative intent into suitable reference assets, generation instructions, and editing operations. Continue from the user's requirements, established visual plan, and existing assets.

## Choose the operation

Choose generation, image editing, image-to-video, video editing, or extension for the problem at hand. When an existing asset is close to the target, consider changing it. Generate anew when a new composition, subject, or direction is needed.

For precise poses, performance, or camera movement, use images or video that express those requirements through controls the available tool supports.

Plan for how assets will be used. Create and composite exact text, subtitles, corner badges, and graphics in HyperFrames when they need precise presentation, reuse, or independent editing.

## Assign reference roles

State what each input controls: identity, clothing, product appearance, setting, style, composition, pose, action, camera movement, or sound. One asset can serve several purposes; identify the features to use and resolve conflicts between references.

Distinguish reference sheets from actual shot frames. A character sheet or storyboard can guide generation; an image-to-video first frame must depict the shot's actual opening composition.

## Describe the change

For an image, describe the intended picture. For an edit, identify what changes and what stays. For video, describe what happens, how it happens, and how the camera presents it, including sequence, direction, speed, contact, and reaction where relevant.

Express abstract intent through visible or audible behavior: hesitation can be a hand stopping short of a door handle; impact can be a landing that shakes nearby objects. Let text supply changes and important constraints that the inputs do not already express clearly.

## Scope the generation

Fit actions to the shot's duration and complexity. Use keyframes, motion references, or separate generations for complex performances, multiple state changes, or precise handoffs.

When exploring, vary meaningful choices such as framing, action, camera movement, or visual treatment. Once a direction is established, produce toward it rather than expanding candidates without a purpose.

## Respond to the result

Inspect the generated asset and identify concrete differences from the intended result. Preserve effective content and choose an operation that addresses the problem: image editing for appearance or composition, revised motion instructions or references for performance, a local video edit or replacement for a faulty section, or separate audio editing for sound.

Each further generation should have a purpose: correct an observed defect or explore a specific alternative.
